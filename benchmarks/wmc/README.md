# Weighted CNF (*.cnf*)

Inputs of the CNF benchmark with the option `-q wmc`. They encode the nine Bayesian networks of
the bnlearn repository (https://www.bnlearn.com/bnrepository/) that Roulette (Moy et al., PLDI
2025, Table 2) is evaluated on, as weighted CNFs generated by the script
`benchmark/BDD/bn/bif2cnf.py` of the Aytdd repository with the options `--prune ancestors
--query-only`. Each file encodes one leaf variable of its network together with the ancestors of
that leaf, and adds the unit clause of one value of the leaf, so that its count is the marginal
probability of that value. Each file names its network, the URL and the SHA-256 of its source,
the query, the pruning and the expected count in its leading comments.

The counts are exact. They are finite decimals, since the tables of the networks hold decimals,
and the comment `c expected wmc` of each file gives all the digits (up to 143 decimal places for
*hepar2*). The generator computes them by variable elimination over the tables of the kept
nodes, in exact arithmetic.

## Pruning

The file of a network keeps its leaf and the ancestors of the leaf and drops every other node.
The count of the file is the marginal probability of the queried value in the sub-network of the
kept nodes. The marginal in the whole network also sums out the dropped nodes, which contributes
the factor 1 when the rows of their tables all sum to 1, and the two then agree, as on six of the
nine networks. In *alarm*, *hepar2* and *munin* dropped nodes have rows that do not sum to 1 in
the source, and the two counts differ. The comment `c unpruned expected wmc` of these files gives
the marginal in the whole network as well.

## Encoding

Every value of every network variable has an indicator variable, constrained by an at-least-one
clause and pairwise at-most-one clauses. Every entry `Pr(x | u)` of a conditional probability
table strictly between 0 and 1 has a parameter variable implied by the conjunction of the
indicators of `x` and of the parent values `u`, written as the single clause of this
implication. An entry equal to 0 instead gets the clause that excludes the conjunction, and an
entry equal to 1 gets neither a variable nor a clause. A parameter variable has the weight
`Pr(x | u)` and its negation the weight `1 - Pr(x | u)`, and both literals of every indicator
have the weight 1. The clause form is that of ENC3 of

> Mark Chavira and Adnan Darwiche. "On probabilistic inference by weighted model counting". In:
> *Artificial Intelligence* 172 (6-7). 2008

(section 5.1), and the weights follow the convention of the model counting competition, in which
the two weights of a variable sum to 1 or are both 1. For an assignment of the indicators that
selects one value of every network variable, the parameter variable of each selected entry is
forced true and contributes its entry, and every other one is free and contributes the sum of its
two weights, which is 1. The weighted model count of the clauses without the query is therefore
the sum over the joint assignments of the products of their table entries, which is 1 when every
row of every table sums to 1, and the unit clause of the indicator of the queried value makes it
the marginal probability of that value.

The variables are numbered node by node in a topological order of the kept nodes, the indicators
of a node before its parameter variables, and this numbering is the variable order, from the top
of the decision diagram down. The files carry no order lines, the comment lines holding a single
number with which the files of [benchmarks/cnf](../cnf) give their order.

## Weights

The weights follow the format of the model counting competition 2022. The line `c t wmc` states
that the file is an instance of weighted model counting, and the line `c p weight L W 0` gives the
literal `L`, a variable or its negation, the weight `W`, a decimal with an optional fraction and
an optional exponent. These lines may come before or after the line `p cnf`, and a literal
without one has the weight 1. The line `c expected wmc V` gives the expected count `V`, a decimal
of the same form, with which the benchmark compares its count. The benchmark reads each weight
exactly as a rational, and as the `double` nearest to it for the packages that count in double
precision. These files give both literals of every variable a weight, after the clauses, and
write every weight as a plain decimal, without an exponent. One entry of *insurance*, 4.999825e-05
in the network, and its complement need 11 decimals, beyond the nine of the competition's
instances. Readers of arbitrary precision take them exactly, and a reader in double precision
rounds them.

The count of RSDD sums over the paths to true of a BDD the products of the weights of the
literals on each path, so a variable that a path skips contributes the factor 1 rather than the
sum of its two weights. On these files it is the weighted model count. No path to true skips an
indicator, since the at-least-one and at-most-one clauses of its network variable make it decide
the path, and a path skips only free parameter variables, whose two weights sum to 1.
